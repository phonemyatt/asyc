# Links replace folders as organizational mechanism

In modern knowledge management, links serve as the primary organizational tool, replacing traditional folder hierarchies. This shift recognizes that knowledge doesn't fit neatly into single categories—most ideas relate to multiple contexts simultaneously.

When a note about "compound interest" relates to both "investing" and "mathematics," folder-based organization forces an artificial choice: does it belong in /Finance or /Math? With link-based organization, the note exists once and links bidirectionally to both contexts. The relationship is explicit through links rather than implicit through folder location.

Maps of Content (MOCs) formalize this approach. An MOC is simply an index note that links to related notes on a topic. Unlike folders, MOCs are themselves notes, which means:
- A note can appear in multiple MOCs without duplication
- MOCs can link to other MOCs, creating flexible hierarchies
- You can add context and narrative around groupings
- MOCs are searchable and can have their own metadata

The practical workflow: create notes freely without worrying about their "correct" location, then build MOCs as navigation hubs when a topic accumulates enough related notes. This bottom-up approach matches how knowledge actually develops—through accumulation and discovery rather than pre-planned categorization.

Folders still serve a purpose for workflow stages (Inbox, Projects, Archive) but not for subject matter organization. The content organization happens through the link graph.
